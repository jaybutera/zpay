Run a taker

Every zpay payment has a sender who puts up ZEC and a taker who sends the dollars. The taker holds the second key on the sender's Zcash escrow, pays the Venmo handle from its own account, proves the payment, and collects the ZEC. The coordinator does the watching, paying, proving and broadcasting. You supply a Zcash node, a key, a Venmo balance, and a browser signed into Venmo. Today the only taker is zpay's own; this page is what it runs.

How the escrow settles Who holds what

What a taker does

A sender types a Venmo handle into the app, and the app asks the coordinator for an escrow. The coordinator answers with a transparent Zcash address whose script has two branches: before a deadline it spends with the sender's signature and yours together, and after the deadline it spends with the sender's alone. Your key is the second one on the first branch. From the moment the sender funds that address, the coordinator:

  1. Finds the funding output, by walking blocks from a Zcash node or by one address-index call on a zcashd with the index on.
  2. Waits for the depth the amount needs: ten confirmations under $50, thirty up to $500, a hundred above that.
  3. Announces the escrow to the attestor, which draws a one-time nonce for this trade. Nothing is committed yet, but the nonce is drawn once and never redrawn.
  4. Takes the sender's pre-signature from the app and verifies it against the sender's key and the attestor's outcome point. This is the gate. Nothing is paid before it passes.
  5. Pays the Venmo handle from a browser tab you already signed in, driven over the Chrome DevTools Protocol, after writing a journal line that says it is about to.
  6. Proves the payment: zk-p2p's Nitro enclave replays your Venmo feed and signs the amount, payee and time. The attestor checks that signature against its own view of the escrow and answers with the outcome scalar.
  7. Releases the ZEC. The scalar decrypts the sender's pre-signature, your key signs the other half, and the coordinator broadcasts. The coins land at your payout address, less the fee lines the quote showed.

Steps one through four cost nothing to abandon. Step five is the one that moves money, and the driver module that runs this sequence is the only place in the coordinator that can. Every step after it records what it did before doing it, so a crash mid-trade resumes rather than pays twice.

The coordinator pays one trade at a time, so the Venmo balance is the throughput limit, not the stake. A $6 cap with $5.50 in the account serves one payment and then refuses until the ZEC is sold and the dollars are back. Set the cap to what the account can actually cover.

Deadlines

Every deadline is measured from the refund height burned into the escrow script, because that height is the only clock the script honours. The refund branch opens 1,152 blocks after the lock, about 24 hours at 75-second blocks. The coordinator refuses to send dollars once the chain is within 60 blocks of that height, about 75 minutes, and it must have the release broadcast 40 blocks before it. Both margins exist so the release confirms before the sender's refund can.

Before the deadline the coordinator will not pay until the funding is deep enough that unwinding it would cost more than the trade. The tiers are read off the dollar amount: ten confirmations up to $50, thirty from $50 to $500, a hundred over $500. A hundred blocks is the depth at which zcashd and zebrad refuse a reorg outright.

What you need

  • A Zcash node. zcashd or zebrad, or a hosted provider that keys on a header. The block scanner works on any of them; the address-index scanner needs zcashd with addressindex=1. Give the key to the process through the environment, not the config file.
  • A secp256k1 key in ZECP2P_LP_PRIV. Its public half goes into every escrow this coordinator opens, so losing it strands every open trade at the refund. A payout address, transparent or shielded, is separate: the key signs, the address only receives.
  • An attestor. The outcome signer is its own daemon with its own key and its own view of the chain. It never sees an escrow key or a sighash; the one thing it emits is a scalar that releases exactly one escrow. Run it beside the coordinator, on the same network and provider, and back up its key file. The app pins the attestor key it trusts on mainnet, so a coordinator that announces a different attestor is refused by the page; a taker with its own attestor serves its own copy of the page with its own pin.
  • Chrome with remote debugging on, signed into Venmo, running headed. Headless Chrome asking for account.venmo.com gets a DataDome interstitial with no login form; under Xvfb the same profile gets the real page. The coordinator attaches to the tab and never sees your password.
  • A Venmo session capture for the enclave, plus your numeric Venmo sender id. The capture is what the enclave replays; the id is how it finds your side of the feed.
  • A Venmo balance that covers the cap. The taker fronts dollars and is repaid in ZEC, so it needs no ZEC of its own before the first trade.

Configure

Secrets come from the environment: the LP key and the attestor's bearer token. Everything else is one TOML file. The lines below are the ones that decide money; the example config in the repository carries the rest with a comment on each. 

# config.v2coordinator.toml
[server]
host = "127.0.0.1"
port = 3000
journal_path = "/home/you/.zecp2p/fills.jsonl"  # the payment slot; absolute path

[zec]
rpc_url = "http://127.0.0.1:8232"
network = "main"
refund_hours = 24
block_seconds = 75
scanner = "block_scan"    # or address_index on zcashd

[attestor]
url = "http://127.0.0.1:8480"
pubkey = "02…"           # required on mainnet

[lp]
payout_address = "t1…"
key_env = "ZECP2P_LP_PRIV"

[quote]
spread_bps = 50           # your margin over the market price
fee_bps = 15              # the platform fee; 0 turns the output off
max_payment_cents = 2500  # refuses above this, never clamps
min_zat = 120000
quote_seconds = 300
max_open_orders = 200
max_open_per_handle = 5

[serve]
handles = []              # who you will pay; empty refuses everyone
allow_any_handle = false
live_payments = false     # false stops at the send button

[venmo]
cdp_url = "http://127.0.0.1:9222"
session_path = "venmo-session.json"
session_max_age_hours = 12
sender_id = ""            # the numeric id, not the handle

Three of those lines refuse rather than default. An empty serve.handles refuses every order, because silence about who to pay must not mean anyone. A missing attestor.pubkey on mainnet refuses to start, because the coordinator relays the attestor's announcement to the page, and an unpinned key would let it name an attestor whose scalar it holds. And the price comes from the market, Coinbase first and Kraken second, cached for 45 seconds; a feed that cannot be read, or that has moved more than half from the last good price, means no quote at all rather than a stale one.

Check before paying

The coordinator checks its config, its node, its attestor and its key and exits, without opening an order: 

zecp2p-v2coordinator --check

With live_payments = false it goes further. It serves orders, watches funding, announces, verifies the pre-signature, opens the Venmo page, fills the amount, reads it back out of the field, and stops at the button. Every step of a real payment runs except the click. The browser driver has the same stop on its own, for a stale selector or a React field that discards its input: 

zecp2p-taker check-venmo                                # a usable, signed-in Venmo tab?
zecp2p-taker test-pay --recipient alice --amount 1.50   # drive the page, stop before send

A build with the test rails feature accepts --simulate-fiat, which settles an escrow against a payment nobody made. It refuses a mainnet network outright, and exists so the release path can be exercised on a test network without a Venmo balance.

Run

ZECP2P_LP_PRIV=… ZECP2P_ATTESTOR_TOKEN=… zecp2p-v2coordinator --config config.v2coordinator.toml

It sweeps open orders every 60 seconds, which is under the 75-second block time on purpose: a sweep can notice a block late, but cannot step over one. zpay runs the coordinator, the attestor and the Chrome as user-level systemd units, with Chrome under Xvfb in its own profile directory so the session survives restarts. That profile is not shared with any browser a person uses. The driver picks the first tab whose URL contains venmo.com and drives it to the Pay button, and an unattended money click does not belong in a browser someone else has open.

Set live_payments = true only after a dry trade has stopped at the button with the right amount in the field. From then on there is no human at the click; the cap, the handle allowlist, the pre-signature check and the journal are what stand between a bad value and a real payment.

The proof

A Venmo payment is not a chain event, so the release needs two signatures from outside the chain. The first is zk-p2p's: the coordinator encrypts your Venmo session to a key inside an AWS Nitro enclave, whose attestation document it checks against the AWS root, and the enclave replays 

GET https://account.venmo.com/api/stories?feedType=me&externalId=<SENDER_ID>

from inside and signs what it finds. The signature is EIP-712 over the domain of the verifier at 0xC6F4…502B on Base, because that is the domain the enclave was built to sign. Nothing is submitted to Base. The domain constants are used only so the signature can be checked.

The second signature is the attestor's. It takes the enclave's attestation and the trade's terms, checks the escrow on its own node, checks the payment is no older than its own announcement, and only then signs the outcome. The scalar it returns completes the sender's pre-signature for this escrow and no other: the outcome point commits to the terms, so a scalar signed for a $1 trade cannot release an escrow that promised $100.

the timestamp bites

The attestor bounds the payment's recency against the moment it issued the announcement, never against a time the coordinator supplied. And the coordinator appends a short per-order tag to the Venmo note and records what it typed, so the feed search finds this payment and not an earlier one of the same amount to the same handle.

The browser and the cookie

The session the enclave replays is a live Venmo credential. It is encrypted to the enclave's attested key before it leaves your machine, so nobody outside the enclave reads it, but it is equal in power to being logged in as you. The coordinator writes it with mode 0600, never logs it, and refuses to pay on material older than session_max_age_hours.

  • Venmo expires an idle session on its own. Measured on 2026-09-05: a session captured at 01:14 UTC was gone from the browser by 04:19 UTC with Chrome never restarted. zpay keeps a keepalive touching the tab every 20 minutes.
  • There is no captcha-free re-login. A signed-out browser sent to account.venmo.com lands on a sign-in page with hCaptcha and reCAPTCHA ahead of the two-factor step, so nothing on the machine can sign back in. A person does that, in the same Chrome.
  • Venmo ties the session to the browser's User-Agent closely enough that the enclave's replay can fail on a mismatch. Chrome's UA and the coordinator's are pinned to the same string.
  • A signed-out tab is caught before money moves. The rail reports it as "cannot start" and the order waits; it does not fail the trade, and the sender's refund stays on offer.

One payment at a time

There is one Venmo balance and one feed, and two payments of the same amount to the same handle cannot be told apart by the enclave's payment index. So the coordinator holds one payment slot, and the slot lives in the journal file rather than in memory: a line is written before the click, and a crash between the click and the record leaves the slot claimed rather than silently free. Every process that pays from this Venmo account must share that file. Both daemons take an exclusive lock on it and make the decision and the claim in one step.

The rule the journal enforces is that no write may bury a line another process is standing on. It took ten audit rounds to hold. The round that found the last double-payment path ran two payers against one journal and watched the second one's claim land over the first one's open payment line.

When it goes wrong

If you cannot pay before the deadline, do not. The sender's key refunds the escrow at the refund height with nobody's help, and the order is marked unpaid. If the funding is reorganised out from under an order, the coordinator fails the order and never pays it; the pre-signature commits to the funding outpoint, so a replaced funding cannot be released either way.

If you paid and cannot get the proof, the dollars are gone and the ZEC still refunds to the sender at the deadline. The coordinator marks the order as needing an operator and holds the slot until a person records what happened. This is the taker's risk, and the reason the allowlist exists.

If the release broadcasts and does not confirm within 40 blocks of the refund height, the sender's refund competes with it. The release carries no expiry height for exactly this case: a release delayed in the mempool is still valid when it is mined.

Paying yourself

The coordinator does not care whose handle it pays. Put your own Venmo username in serve.handles, point the app at your coordinator, and send ZEC to the escrow it opens. Your coordinator pays your own Venmo, proves it, and takes the ZEC back to your payout address. Your ZEC becomes dollars in your Venmo with nobody else in the loop, and the spread and fee are yours to set. 

https://zpay.cash/app/?api=https://coordinator.example

The app remembers the coordinator it was pointed at. Your coordinator must list the app's origin in allowed_origins, or the browser blocks the calls; empty means same-origin only.

This is the shape of the first mainnet payment, on 2026-09-05: $2.01 sent to a Venmo handle, attested by the enclave, signed by the attestor, and released in transaction b39c8c66…2424. Nothing in that run was simulated. The coordinator drove every step.
