how it works

One address, one proof, one key

Your ZEC locks in an escrow on Zcash. A payer sees it, sends the Venmo, and proves it through the same enclave zk-p2p uses. That proof is the only thing that can release the escrow. If nobody pays within a day, your own key alone takes the coins back. Cashing out to yourself is the same flow with your own handle.

01

Type their handle, get an address

Enter their Venmo username and an amount. The app makes a key in your browser and never sends it anywhere. The address it derives is an escrow whose refund branch only that key can spend, and the app checks that before it shows you anything.

custody

Who holds what, and for how long

Three things touch value between your wallet and their Venmo. What each one can and cannot do is read off the escrow script, not off a promise.

The escrow

A script on Zcash with two ways out. Your key and the payer's key together spend it into the release. Your key alone spends it after the timeout. There is no third key, no admin, and nothing to upgrade.

The app derives the address from your key and refuses any address it cannot derive itself, so the address you fund is one whose refund branch you control.

Holds: your ZEC from confirmation until release or refund. Escape hatch: the refund, signed by you alone after 24 h.

The payer

Sends the dollars from their own Venmo to the handle you typed, fiat first. The release they hold is a pre-signature you prepared, and it only becomes a valid spend once the enclave's attestation is folded in. Until then it moves nothing.

Holds: nothing of yours. Risk they carry: their dollars are out before the release lands.

The proof

A Venmo payment is not an on-chain event, so the release is gated on an attestation from zk-p2p's Nitro enclave, which replays the payer's Venmo feed and signs the amount, the payee hash and the time. The attestor is consulted, not trusted with keys: it cannot spend the escrow, and it cannot sign a refund.

Holds: nothing. Trusted for: saying a payment happened. Without it, no release.

What zpay does not do yet

A payer has a day

The escrow is refundable 24 hours after it locks. If nobody has paid by then, the app signs the refund with your key and the order is over. Small orders are the ones payers skip, so they are the ones most likely to come back.

Keep the link

The key that refunds your ZEC lives in the app's status link and in your browser, nowhere else. Lose both before the order settles and nobody, zpay included, can sign the refund. The app tells you to copy the link before it shows the address.

Their handle is not private

The escrow commits to a hash of the recipient's Venmo username, and the payer learns the handle in order to pay it. Treat the handle, amount and time of a payment as known to the payer and the enclave. The escrow itself is a transparent t3 address, so the amount and the deposit are on the public chain; spend from a shielded pool if you care about the input.

Refunds land on a transparent address

The refund is a plain transparent spend to an address you type. Shield it afterwards from your wallet.

Leave the page open until it says locked

The release pre-signature is made in your browser after the deposit confirms. If the page is closed at that moment, open the status link again and it picks up where it left off; until it does, no payer will send the dollars.

liquidity

Be the payer. Keep the spread.

Every locked escrow is ZEC that goes to whoever pays its Venmo and proves it. Pay from your own account, present the enclave's attestation, and the release lands on your address. The daemon watches, pays and proves from a browser you are already logged into, and stops before each money step unless you tell it not to.
