    how it works

    Two legs, one contract, one proof

    The swap leg turns ZEC into USDC on Base. The payment leg turns that USDC into a Venmo transfer. zpay's contract sits between them and only ever pays out against a proof.

    01

    Name a handle, get an address

    Enter your Venmo username and a ZEC amount. zpay registers the handle with zk-p2p's curator and opens a session whose payee is fixed to that hash. You get one Zcash deposit address.

    @handle→payee hash→session
    02

    Send ZEC, it lands as USDC

    NEAR Intents' 1Click route swaps your ZEC to USDC on Base and delivers it to the zpay contract, which posts it as a sell order on zk-p2p's escrow. The order pays only the handle from step one; the contract reverts on any other payee.

    ZEC→USDC→escrow
    03

    A taker pays you, then proves it

    A taker stakes, sends the dollars from their own Venmo, and asks zk-p2p's Nitro enclave to attest the payment. The on-chain verifier checks that signature and releases the USDC to them. By then the dollars are already in your Venmo.

    Venmo→enclave attestation→release
    custody

    Who holds what, and for how long

    Three parties touch value during an offramp. What each one can and cannot do is read off the contract and the deploy, not off a promise.

    The contract

    Deployed once, no proxy, no upgrade path. The only privileged action its owner has is naming the keeper, and the keeper can only open sessions and post your credited USDC to escrow. It cannot change the payee, and it cannot pay itself.

    Your own key can pull the USDC back at any point before a taker claims it. Nobody else's signature is needed.

    Holds: USDC between arrival and the escrow deposit. Escape hatch: rescue and withdraw, signed by you.

    The swap

    1Click custodies your ZEC from the moment it leaves your wallet until USDC lands on the contract. That window is not trustless. If the route fails, 1Click refunds to the transparent Zcash address you gave on the quote's deadline.

    Holds: ZEC in flight, minutes. Escape hatch: the refund address on the quote.

    The proof

    A Venmo payment is not an on-chain event, so the escrow releases only against a signature from zk-p2p's Nitro enclave, which replays the taker's Venmo feed and attests the amount, the payee hash and the timestamp. The verifier contract checks that signature during release.

    Holds: nothing. Trusted for: saying a payment happened. Without it, no release.

    limits

    What zpay does not do yet

    Better to read this here than to find it after sending.

    Venmo only, for now

    The coordinator registers Venmo payees. The enclave that signs proofs knows eleven platforms, and each one needs its own payee registration and payment driver before it turns on here.

    VenmoCash AppPayPalZelleWiseRevolutMonzoChimeMonobankAlipayUPI

    Base mainnet only

    zk-p2p's Venmo verifier is deployed on Base and nowhere else, and the enclave binds every attestation to chain 8453. There is no testnet version of the payment leg.

    Orders wait for a taker

    Nothing bounds how long a sell order sits in escrow. Small orders wait longest, because a taker pays the same gas and locks their stake for fourteen days whether the order is $5 or $500. You can withdraw any time before a taker claims it.

    Your handle is not private

    The escrow stores a hash of your Venmo username, and zk-p2p's curator answers that hash to anyone who asks. Treat the handle, amount and time of an order as public. The Zcash side ends at a transparent deposit address, so spend from a shielded pool if you care about the input.

    A floor, not a minimum that fills

    1Click refuses anything under 0.00052 ZEC. Above that, whether an order fills is up to takers, and the smallest orders are the ones they skip.

    One session at a time per contract

    The contract keeps every session's USDC in one pot and credits arrivals to one open session, so the coordinator serves offramps in series. If yours is queued, the app says so.
